        Method and sources

        What changed from the original

        This version was rebuilt from an audit of the original Headroom site, which is kept for comparison at /original. The audit write-ups are in the project repository.

        • Meetings is split in two. Live meetings and discussion stay a human task type; meeting prep, notes and follow-up is its own type, scored like other codified writing work. A quarter of each job's meetings time is treated as prep and follow-up. This adds under a point to today's figure.
        • Forecast ranges stop at today's score and at the ceiling, since scores do not fall and cannot pass the ceiling.
        • Every snapshot highlight links to its sources, and the source tiers are defined by who ran the evaluation.
        • Corrections from three source checks: METR horizons and doubling time, the OSWorld and τ²-bench airline figures, Mercor's wording, the frontier on each snapshot date, and primary pages in place of aggregators for GDPval-AA and τ²-bench telecom.
        • A Value page that sets the frontier labs' revenue against the white-collar wage bill and projects it as models can do more of the work, more of that work is handed to them, and labs earn a share of what it is worth, with adjustable scenarios.
        • Reading aids: a definition under the headline, the level at saturation, a biggest-gains list, a pace check beside the forecasts, a colour scheme where blue is AI capability and clay is the work that needs a person, and tooltips that explain and highlight each band, step through a task type's history and open the evidence behind each snapshot.

        Snapshots

        One snapshot per Claude Opus release. Each scores what the best publicly available model could do on that date, which is not always the Opus model itself. Earlier snapshots were scored afterwards from benchmark results and are marked as backfilled. Abbreviations: GDPval, expert-graded comparison with professionals' deliverables; METR, length of task a model finishes on its own; RLI, Remote Labor Index, share of whole freelance projects done to professional standard; OSWorld, computer use.

        Checking the forecasts

        Each live snapshot's Frontier+1 and Frontier+2 forecasts are kept when it is retired, so the next snapshots can be scored against them.

        Sources

        Primary: a model's own lab, or the paper or study that produced the figure. Independent: a third party that runs its own benchmark or evaluation on other companies' models. Secondary: news, aggregators and blogs relaying someone else's figures. Each task type on the Tasks tab links to its own sources.
