# Frontier models absorb codified desk work first

Measured against Headroom's standard (work a competent professional would accept after light review, using tools available at the time), the evidence supports a steep but uneven climb. Between Claude Opus 4.1 (August 2025) and Opus 5.5 (September 2026), this report scores the share of task time frontier models could do as rising from **44 to 76 on the 15 codified information task types**, from **24 to 49 on the seven judgment-heavy types**, and only from **6 to 13 on the seven interpersonal and physical types** (unweighted means of the Appendix A scores). Three measured series anchor those numbers: expert-graded GDPval parity rose from 47.6% to about 85% by April 2026; METR's 80%-reliability time horizon rose from 23 minutes to about 3 hours by its last measurement in spring 2026; and the Remote Labor Index's whole-project automation rate rose from 2.5% to 21.25%. The definition deliberately sits between saturated narrow benchmarks (tau2 telecom 99%, OSWorld-Verified 83% and rising) and end-to-end automation (RLI 21%; strict OSWorld 2.0 full-completion rates about 35 points below partial credit), and occupation scores built from the task scores land within about ten points of ESADE's October 2026 O*NET-wide estimate (37.8% of the average occupation's time replaceable; 56–71% in the legal, office, business and computing groups) for five of seven test occupations. If gaps keep closing at the pace of the last ten months, which is consistent with METR's ~4.3-month doubling and Epoch's 14 capability-index points per year, codified work reaches about **84 at Frontier+1 (April 2027) and 89 at Frontier+2 (October 2027)**, judgment work 58 and 65, and interpersonal work only 17 and 20. Ceilings are set by physical presence, legal sign-off and human preference rather than intelligence, and run from 94–99 for informational tasks down to 15–55 for hands-on, care, management and relationship work. Confidence is highest for coding, drafting and data entry and lowest for sales, scheduling, compliance, bookkeeping and translation, where no 2026 benchmark with model scores exists; METR has published no time horizon for any Anthropic model since Mythos Preview in April 2026.

## Thirteen months moved expert parity from 48% to the mid-80s

Anthropic shipped seven Opus releases in the window at a quickening pace: 4.1 (August 5, 2025), 4.5 (November 24), 4.6 (February 5, 2026), 4.7 (April 16), 4.8 (May 28), 5 (July 24) and 5.5 (September 22), roughly one every two months in 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_(language_model)); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/24/anthropic-launches-opus-5/)). **Opus was not always the frontier.** Anthropic's gated Claude Mythos Preview (April 7) led 17 of the 18 benchmarks Anthropic measured while Opus 4.7 and 4.8 were current ([R&D World](https://www.rdworldonline.com/claude-mythos-leads-17-of-18-benchmarks-anthropic-measured-muse-spark-put-meta-back-in-the-frontier-club-and-openais-spud-model-is-reportedly-near-launch/)). From June 9 the public Claude Fable 5 and gated Mythos 5 sat above Opus 5, which Anthropic said "comes close to the frontier intelligence of Claude Fable 5 at half the price" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/24/anthropic-launches-opus-5/)). OpenAI's GPT-5.2, GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.5 led OpenAI's own GDPval from December through April. Opus 5.5 restored Opus to the top: it is **#1 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 58** and tied with GPT-6 Astra at **169 on Epoch's Capabilities Index (ECI)** ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models); [Epoch](https://epochai.substack.com/p/the-epoch-brief-october-23-2026)). Each Appendix A column therefore scores the best publicly available capability on that Opus release date, excluding gated models that were not "reasonably available." In practice the Opus model sets most rows; where a public peer led a task type, the score follows the leader, a difference of at most a few points.

Three series carry most of the weight. On **GDPval**, OpenAI's blind expert grading of deliverables from 44 occupations, the best model went from **47.6% wins-or-ties (Opus 4.1)** to 70.9% (GPT-5.2, December 2025), **80.3% (Opus 4.7)** and **84.9% (GPT-5.5, April 2026)** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04374); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5-5/)). The win-rate metric then neared its noise ceiling, since with 220 gold tasks results within ±6.6 points of parity cannot be told apart ([testmuai](https://www.testmuai.com/learning-hub/gdpval/)). Artificial Analysis's Elo-based GDPval-AA took over, and Opus 5.5 leads it at **1846–1866, 138 points above Opus 5** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5); [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/gdpval-aa)). On **METR's time horizon**, the length of software task a model completes at a given reliability, the 50% horizon rose from **1h40m (Opus 4.1) to 4h53m (Opus 4.5), 11h59m (Opus 4.6) and about 17h (Mythos Preview)**, while the 80% horizon moved from **23 minutes to 49 minutes, 70 minutes and 3h06m** (METR's results file after its March 2026 correction; the pre-correction figures of 5h20m and 42 minutes for Opus 4.5 still circulate) ([METR results file](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml)). METR has stopped updating its page, says measurements above 16 hours are unreliable, and has published no horizon for Opus 5 or 5.5 ([METR](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)). On the **Remote Labor Index (RLI)**, which asks whether a whole freelance project deliverable is at least as good as the professional's, the top score climbed from **2.5% at launch (October 2025) to 3.75% (Opus 4.5), 8.33% (Opus 4.8), 15.8% (Fable 5) and 21.25% (Opus 5.5)** ([Scale](https://scale.com/blog/rli); [Scale Labs](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/rli); [CAIS](https://safe.ai/blog/significant-increase-in-digital-labor-automation)).

Narrow agentic benchmarks moved faster, then saturated and were replaced by versions that break comparability. OSWorld-Verified (computer use) rose from 66.3% (Opus 4.5) to 83.4% (Opus 4.8), and GPT-5.4 passed the 72.4% human baseline with 75.0% in March 2026 ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5-4/); [LLM Stats](https://llm-stats.com/blog/research/claude-opus-4-8-launch)). SWE-bench Verified reached 87.6% (Opus 4.7) and 93.9% (Mythos Preview) before vendors dropped it for SWE-bench Pro, where Opus went 53.4% → 64.3% → 69.2% → 79.2% from 4.6 to 5 ([computingforgeeks](https://computingforgeeks.com/claude-opus-4-7-released-features-benchmarks/); [DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/claude-opus-5)). Because Terminal-Bench 2.0, 2.1 and 4.0 and OSWorld-Verified, 2.0 and 2.1 are not comparable across versions, the 2026 snapshots rely on how much of the remaining gap closed within one version rather than on raw levels. Almost every launch figure is vendor-reported; the independent anchors are METR, Artificial Analysis, Vals AI, Scale's RLI and Epoch.

## Light review sits between a 21% floor and a 90% benchmark ceiling

No benchmark measures Headroom's definition directly, so the scores triangulate three tiers of evidence. **Narrow pass rates are upper bounds**: tau2 telecom customer-service agents score 99.25% (Opus 4.6), OSWorld-Verified reached 83.4% (Opus 4.8), well past the 72.4% human baseline, and SpreadsheetBench V1 sits at 98–99% ([Anthropic Opus 4.6 system card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/0dd865075ad3132672ee0ab40b05a53f14cf5288.pdf); [LLM Stats](https://llm-stats.com/blog/research/claude-opus-4-8-launch); [bettersheets](https://bettersheets.co/blog/best-ai-for-spreadsheets)). **Expert-parity rates on well-specified deliverables are the central anchor**, because GDPval wins-or-ties map closely onto "competent professional quality," although GDPval tasks arrive fully specified with their input files and so leave out the work of gathering context. **End-to-end rates are the floor**: RLI's 21.25% requires a whole project with no human fix-up, the best model's APEX-Agents Pass@1 was 38.4% in April 2026 ([Mercor](https://x.com/mercor/status/2048095912287977603)), and Epoch concluded in October 2026 that models "cannot yet replace workers, at least not at Epoch" ([Epoch](https://epochai.substack.com/p/the-epoch-brief-october-23-2026)).

Turning "can sometimes" into "can, with light review" is a reliability problem, and two measured gaps size it. **METR's 80% horizon is one-fifth to one-tenth of the 50% horizon** (3h06m vs at least 16h for Mythos Preview; 70 minutes vs 12 hours for Opus 4.6), and **Anthropic's strict full-completion rates on OSWorld 2.0 run about 36 points below partial credit** (Fable 5.1: 77.9% partial, 41.7% strict; Opus 5.5 reports 81.8% partial and no strict score) ([benchmarklist](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/osworld_2_0_anthropic/)). GDPval's own cost analysis shows why partial success earns almost no credit: once human review and redoing are counted, GPT-5's raw 90–327x speedup shrank to **1.12–1.39x** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04374)). The scores therefore count only time where the model's output would survive light review. Expert-parity rates are scaled down where a task's natural unit runs longer than the 80% horizon and pulled toward the RLI floor for multi-day, multimodal projects, while short, well-specified units (an email, a form, a contained code change) score close to the parity rate.

Tools matter because GDPval-style tasks hand over context that real work makes the model fetch. In API usage each 1% more input context comes with 0.38% more output, and Anthropic calls context access a bottleneck for enterprise deployment ([AEI Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report)). Integration widened sharply during the window, with 1M-token context and agent teams arriving with Opus 4.6, Claude Cowork in January 2026, Dispatch in March and Claude in Chrome generally available in August ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_(language_model))). I therefore apply a context discount on desk tasks of roughly 10–15% of the parity rate for the 2025 snapshots, narrowing to about 5% by Opus 5.5. Put compactly, each score is approximately the parity rate on the task's natural unit, discounted for units beyond the reliable horizon and for missing context, bounded below by end-to-end rates and above by narrow pass rates.

Two independent lenses check the resulting levels. In real usage on November 2025 traffic (the Opus 4.5 and Sonnet 4.5 era), Anthropic measured task success of **67% on Claude.ai and 49% on the API**, falling from about 60% for sub-hour API tasks to 45% for tasks of five hours or more ([AEI Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)); the Opus 4.5 column averages 54 across the 15 codified task types, between the two. ESADE's October 2026 study rated all 24,087 task-activity pairs in O*NET against 33 capability benchmarks and found **37.8% of the average occupation's time replaceable and a further 48.4% augmentable** ([ESADE](https://www.esade.edu/ecpol/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/working_paper_2026-10-22.pdf)). "Light review" falls between ESADE's replace and augment layers, so occupation scores built from Appendix A should land near ESADE's replaceable share. Using illustrative time mixes (my assumptions, not measured allocations), five of seven occupations land within about ten points:

The misses have clear explanations. Physician scores run above ESADE because Appendix A credits diagnosis that a physician reviews lightly, whereas ESADE assigns diagnosis to its "expert complementarity" layer (44.2% of a family physician's time). Teacher and sales scores run below because ESADE's Education group includes postsecondary and library work, and its Sales group includes about 7 million retail salespersons and cashiers ([BLS OEWS](https://www.bls.gov/oes/special-requests/oesm25nat.zip)) whose transactional tasks are far more codified than B2B persuasion. Economy-wide references point the same way: McKinsey estimates agents could technically automate 44% of all U.S. work hours with technology demonstrated in November 2025 ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/de/news/presse/2025-12-02-mgi-agents-and-robots); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/25/why-ai-wont-take-your-job-partnership-agents-robots-mckinsey/)), and Anthropic estimates about half of all work is exposed to language models alone ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/what-work-can-robots-do)). Both totals include physical jobs, so white-collar shares should, and here do, run higher.

## Codified tasks score in the 80s, judgment in the 50s, people work near 10

**The codified cluster averages 76 at Opus 5.5.** Editing (88), data entry (85), drafting (84), coding (82) and routine customer inquiries (82) lead, each backed by benchmarks at or near saturation. Coding carries the strongest trend evidence in the dataset: METR's horizons are measured on software, SWE-bench Pro gained 5–11 points per Opus release through 2026, and secondary coverage of Anthropic's internal study reports about **5x more commits per engineer in six months**, with agent hours outnumbering employee hours 6:1 by August 2026 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic)). Debugging (72), bookkeeping (68), quantitative analysis (66), scheduling (66) and compliance (66) trail because they need zero-error outputs or system access that benchmarks rarely test. Human experts still beat Opus 4.6 on FrontierFinance by **77.2% to 61.8%** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05912)), general models score about **35%** on real-workbook SpreadsheetBench V2 while specialised spreadsheet tools reach 50–55% ([benchmarklist](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/spreadsheetbench_v2/)), and Microsoft's 200,000 Copilot conversations show data analysis among the lowest-success activities ([Microsoft](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07935)).

**The judgment cluster averages 49.** Legal work shows the split most clearly: Opus 4.6 scored **90.2% on BigLaw Bench core tasks** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-6)), but the best model's APEX-Agents law Pass@1 was **29.7%** in March 2026 ([Mercor](https://x.com/mercor/status/2029635191803928828)) and Harvey's research set targets case-law problems "leading models currently cannot solve" ([Snorkel](https://snorkel.ai/biglaw-bench-research/)). Expert analysis therefore scores 56, not 85. METR's Opus 5.5 assessment, which found limited gains in "judgement" or "taste," backs a slower slope for the whole cluster ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-09-22-claude-opus-5-5/)). Visual and media production is the outlier: the lowest-scoring cognitive type (38) but the fastest-moving, since RLI rose about 8.5x in a year even as CAIS judged Fable 5's 2D video ads, 3D/CAD and architecture deliverables unacceptable as finished work ([CAIS](https://safe.ai/blog/significant-increase-in-digital-labor-automation)).

**The interpersonal and physical cluster averages 13 and has no benchmarks at all**, so its scores rest on labor-side evidence. ESADE finds the probability a task is replaceable falls from **70.5% for purely informational tasks to 14.1% when presence is required and 0.5% when manipulation is required**, and identifies "bundling factors" (relational nexus, organisational responsibility and authorship) that keep tasks with a person even where exposure is high ([ESADE](https://www.esade.edu/ecpol/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/working_paper_2026-10-22.pdf)). The model's contribution here is the codified slice of the work, such as drafting outreach, preparing for meetings and writing case notes, which Headroom scores under drafting, editing and document processing instead.

Against the placeholders, the evidence moved most scores up and two down. The largest upward revisions are debugging (45 → 72), coding (62 → 82), technical support (50 → 72), customer inquiries (62 → 82), creative concepting (40 → 58), technical design (35 → 50) and expert analysis (42 → 56). The placeholders roughly matched Opus 4.6-era evidence and missed the 2026 agentic step, in which RLI quintupled between February and September and computer use passed the human baseline. Media falls from 45 to 38 because production deliverables still fail professional acceptance, and translation from 80 to 78 because the type bundles live interpretation and no 2026 benchmark exists. Labor-market data agree with this ordering, though they measure adoption rather than capability. Employment of 22–25-year-olds in highly exposed occupations is **19% below** where it would be had it kept pace with less-exposed peers, with declines concentrated where AI automates rather than complements ([Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)). Anthropic's own junior share of technical hires fell from 27% to 9% ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic)), yet the Yale Budget Lab still finds no clear indication of economy-wide disruption ([Yale Budget Lab](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/what-we-do-and-dont-know-about-how-ai-affecting-labor-market)).

## Trend continuation puts codified work near 89 by October 2027

Frontier+1 lands about seven months after Opus 5.5 and Frontier+2 about thirteen. With an Opus roughly every two months in 2026, each step spans two or three point releases, and three labs already sit within three ECI points of each other at 166–169 ([Epoch](https://epochai.substack.com/p/the-epoch-brief-october-23-2026)). METR's horizon has doubled every **128.7 days (CI 104–158) since 2023** in its results file after the March 2026 correction (130.8 days in the January release of Time Horizon 1.1), a fit that leaves out models above 16 hours such as Mythos Preview ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-01-29-time-horizon-1-1/); [METR results file](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml)), and a secondary fit to post-2024 models gives about 89 days ([METR results file](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml)). Opus 5.5's 80% horizon has not been measured; my extrapolation from Mythos Preview's 3h06m puts it at roughly 4–8 hours. A 4.3-month doubling multiplies that about 3x by F+1 and about 8x by F+2, which would mean on the order of one to three working days of reliable autonomous software work at F+1 and about a working week at F+2, if the trend holds. Epoch's ECI frontier has gained **14 points per year since reasoning models arrived**, versus 6 before ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/eci-frontier-trend)), implying roughly 177 at F+1 and 184 at F+2: a step as large as the frontier's gain over the past year, the year that took RLI from 2.5% to 21%. Frontier training compute is growing 4–5x a year and algorithmic efficiency halves the compute needed for a fixed capability about every eight months ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/blog/training-compute-of-frontier-ai-models-grows-by-4-5x-per-year); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/04/08/1135398/mustafa-suleyman-ai-future/amp/)); Epoch's projected training-duration limit around 2027 would mostly slow generations after F+2 ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/longest-training-run)).

Gap-closing rates are the most directly usable trend. On GDPval each release closed 51%, then 42%, then 11% of the remaining gap as the win-rate metric saturated, while from Opus 5 to 5.5, in two months, Terminal-Bench 4.0 and partial-credit OSWorld 2.0 each closed about 30% of their remaining gaps, AutomationBench 18% and HLE 11% ([Kingy AI](https://kingy.ai/blog/claude-opus-5-5-specs-benchmarks-pricing-comparison/)). The forecasts carry forward the gap-closing pace of the Appendix A scores themselves. From Opus 4.5 to 5.5, over ten months, the scores closed 41–64% of each codified type's gap to its ceiling (median about 54%), 26–43% for judgment types and 12–25% for interpersonal types. Scaled to a six-to-seven-month generation, that gives a closure rate g of about 0.40 for codified types, 0.45 for coding and debugging (where METR's software trend applies directly), 0.25 for judgment types (0.20 for accountability-bound strategy) and 0.10–0.15 for interpersonal and physical types. Each forecast is F+1 = Now + g × (Ceiling − Now), with F+2 applying the same g again. This assumes neither acceleration, despite faster METR doubling and shorter release cycles, nor slowdown, despite GDPval saturation. The result is a codified average of **76 → 84 → 89 against a ceiling of 96**, a judgment average of **49 → 58 → 65 against 86**, and an interpersonal average of **13 → 17 → 20 against 42**. The end-to-end floor should keep rising as well: extending RLI's last year in a straight line (about +1.6 points a month) gives roughly 32% at F+1 and 42% at F+2, while extending its trend in log-odds gives about 50% and 75–80%. Media and project scores stay above either path, as they should, because Headroom allows review and counts sub-tasks.

The risks run both ways. On the downside, METR's suite cannot measure past 16 hours, so the trend after Mythos Preview is unobserved; not every release extends it, as GPT-5.6 Sol measured only about **11h18m** in June, in a pre-deployment run METR itself does not consider robust ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-06-26-gpt-5-6-sol/)); some reliability measures have stalled, with "no 2026 frontier model" beating "the best 2024 model" on package hallucination, although that study's 2026 set is five models released between October 2025 and March 2026 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17062)); and LEAP's experts do not expect 80% success on 8-hour tasks until 2030 ([LEAP](https://leap.forecastingresearch.org/reports/wave8)). On the upside, that same panel's end-2026 median for the best 80% horizon (3.4 hours) was nearly reached in May, the AI Futures Project's most aggressive forecaster places an automated coder in November 2027, right at F+2 ([AI Futures](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/q25-2026-timelines-update-uplift)), and Gemini 4 Argon already claims 51.3% on AutomationBench against Opus 5.5's 40.0% ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/google-unveils-gemini-4-argon-retaking-benchmark-lead-over-openai-and-anthropic-but-in-limited-release)). A fair band is about ±5 points on codified rows at F+1 and ±8 at F+2, wider on judgment rows.

## Bodies, signatures and trust set the ceilings

A ceiling asks what stays human even with unlimited intelligence, and labor-side evidence identifies three binding constraints. Physical presence is the hardest: 46% of U.S. working time is physical, robots are cost-competitive for only 0.3% of tasks, and at a 3% annual cost decline they would need about 40 years to reach 10% ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/what-work-can-robots-do)), while in ESADE's ratings the 37.7% of O*NET task-activity pairs that require manipulation are essentially never replaceable. Accountable sign-off is the second: ESADE rates the most tacit expert judgement (its C4 band) **0% replaceable but 74.4% augmentable**, and its relational-nexus and organisational-responsibility factors are nearly uncorrelated with exposure (|ρ| ≤ 0.13), so they bind even where capability is high; authorship, by contrast, rises with exposure (ρ 0.28 with the replaceable layer), so credit alone does not shield a task ([ESADE](https://www.esade.edu/ecpol/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/working_paper_2026-10-22.pdf)). Human preference is the third: preferences constrain about a quarter of physical tasks and regulation 14% ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/what-work-can-robots-do)), employment holds up where AI complements experienced workers ([Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)), and experienced respondents report about 10 points less AI-doable work than first-year workers ([AEI Jun 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)). Dario Amodei's framing captures the residual: "If you automate 90% of the job, then everyone does the 10% of the job" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/26/sam-altman-dario-amodei-walking-back-ai-jobs-apocalypse-prophecies-ipo/)).

Most ceiling changes from the placeholders are small. Translation drops from 97 to 94 because the type bundles live clinical and courtroom interpretation, which needs presence. Diagnosis drops from 88 to 85 because licensure keeps the decision with the physician and ESADE places diagnosis in its complementarity layer. Strategy (70 → 65), meetings (60 → 55) and people management (45 → 40) reflect the organisational-responsibility factor and C4's zero replaceability. Creative concepting (92 → 90), technical design (95 → 93), compliance (92 → 90) and project management (93 → 90) each lose a few points for attestation and the preference for a credited human author. The physical, care, teaching, relationship and sales ceilings are kept as given, since the evidence supports their order of magnitude but cannot distinguish 30 from 35 or 70 from 75.

## Conclusion

The binding constraint has moved from intelligence to reliability and context. Opus 5.5 matches or beats professionals on most well-specified deliverables yet completes only about one in five whole freelance projects, and the five-to-ten-fold gap between METR's 50% and 80% horizons, along with the roughly 36-point gap between partial-credit and strict OSWorld 2.0 scores, is where the next two generations' gains will show up. Those are exactly the quantities Headroom's "light review" standard captures and narrow benchmarks miss. A second implication follows from the arithmetic: by Frontier+2, codified task types sit within about seven points of their ceilings while interpersonal types barely move, so differences between occupations will be decided almost entirely by each occupation's share of time in presence-bound, accountable and relational work rather than by model tier. From 2027 on, the task-mix weights and ceilings, more than the frontier scores, will determine Headroom's occupation rankings, which makes better time-allocation data and independent post-May-2026 horizon measurements the two investments most likely to change the picture.

## Appendix B: Evidence conflicts and gaps that most affect the scores

Several source conflicts remain unresolved. Opus 4.8's Terminal-Bench 2.1 score is reported as 74.6% ([LLM Stats](https://llm-stats.com/blog/research/claude-opus-4-8-launch)) and as 82.7% in later comparison tables ([DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/claude-mythos-5)). Mythos Preview's OSWorld-Verified score is reported as 79.6% and as 85.4%. Opus 4.5's 50% METR horizon was 5h20m before METR's March 2026 regularization correction and is 4h53m (293 minutes) in the current results file, which this report now uses throughout. GDPval-AA values differ across aggregators: BenchLM lists Opus 5 at 1862 and LLM Stats lists Fable 5.1 at 1853, while Anthropic's v2.1 table has 1708 and 1735 ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/gdpvalaa); [LLM Stats](https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/gdpval-aa)). Opus 5's SWE-bench figures (96.0% Verified, 79.2% Pro) reach us only through DataCamp's relay of vendor tables. One aggregator lists Opus 5.5 at 89.9% on SWE-bench Pro, a figure Anthropic did not report, so it is not used here ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/swe-bench-pro)). Mercor's October APEX-Agents leaderboard does not label its metric. Fable 5's RLI is given as 15.8% by CAIS and 16.1% by some secondary reports. OpenAI reports GPT-6 Astra at 72.6% on an OSWorld 2.0 offline subset under its own harness, which is not comparable with Anthropic's numbers and reaches this report only through secondary coverage.

The gaps matter more than the conflicts. No GDPval wins-or-ties figure exists for Opus 4.5, 4.6, 4.8, 5 or 5.5, so those columns lean on GDPval-AA Elo, which cannot be converted directly into a task share. No METR horizon exists for Opus 4.7 or any later Anthropic model. No benchmark with published model scores was found for sales or negotiation, scheduling, compliance review, claims processing, bookkeeping, creative concepting, systems design or 2026 translation. Vals publishes only rankings, not scores, for Finance Agent v2.1, LegalBench, Tax Agent Bench and SAGE. No study reports U.S. white-collar time shares by work activity, which is what Headroom's task-to-occupation weights need. On the employment side, the occupation list now covers about 60.5 million of the 87.1 million jobs in the twelve white-collar major groups (BLS OEWS, May 2025). OEWS excludes the self-employed, which undercounts lawyers, real-estate agents, writers, designers, interpreters and financial advisors. The largest omitted white-collar occupations are shipping, receiving and inventory clerks (817,000), short-term substitute teachers (525,000) and hotel desk clerks (261,000); registered nurses (3.4 million) and retail sales fall outside the definition used here ([BLS OEWS](https://www.bls.gov/oes/special-requests/oesm25nat.zip)).
