# Labor-market studies on which occupations and tasks AI can do or is used for (2023 to Oct 2026)

Scope note: U.S. focus. Today is 2026-10-27. Every figure carries its source URL and publication date. "Primary" means read on the publisher's own page; "secondary" means news or blog coverage only, so it is less reliable.

## 1. Anthropic Economic Index (AEI): usage by occupation and task, automation vs. augmentation, API, success and autonomy, geography

### Takeaway
Claude usage is still concentrated in Computer & Mathematical tasks (about 35% of Claude.ai and about 45 to 52% of API traffic). The "directive" (automation) share on Claude.ai rose from 27% to 39% between Jan and Aug 2025, then fell back to 32% by Nov 2025. API/enterprise traffic is mostly automation: about 77% in Aug 2025 and a directive share of 64% in Nov 2025. Measured task success is 67% on Claude.ai and 49% on the API. Success falls as tasks get longer, with a 50% success horizon of about 3.5 hours on the API. About 49% of occupations have had at least a quarter of their tasks performed with Claude. When coverage is weighted by success and time, it overstates real impact for many jobs.

### Cited Findings

**Report timeline (all primary):** Sep 15, 2025 (geography and enterprise API); Jan 15, 2026 ("Economic primitives", Nov 2025 data); Mar 24, 2026 ("Learning curves", Feb 2026 data); Jun 26, 2026 ("Cadences", Apr to Jun 2026 data, first Economic Index Survey). Related studies: Mar 5, 2026 labor-market "observed exposure" paper; Sep 30, 2026 robots index; Oct 16, 2026 "How employment is changing at Anthropic". I found no new AEI flagship report between July and October 2026; there was only a Canada country brief on Jul 14, 2026 — [Anthropic Economics team page](https://www.anthropic.com/research/team/economics)

**Automation vs. augmentation over time (Claude.ai)**
- Jan 2025: augmentation 56%, automation 41% — [AEI Jan 2026 report, Jan 15, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- The directive share (the user hands the whole task to Claude) went 27% (Dec 2024/Jan 2025) → 39% (Aug 2025). Aug 2025 was the first report in which automation exceeded augmentation. Re-classified with Sonnet 4, automation was 49% — [AEI Sep 2025 report, Sep 15, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report)
- Nov 2025: the trend reversed. Augmentation was 52% (up 5pp) and automation 45% (down 4pp). The directive share fell to 32% — [AEI Jan 2026 report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- Feb 2026: augmentation on Claude.ai rose slightly, driven by validation and learning patterns. Automation on the 1P API "decreased sharply." No exact percentages appear in the text — [AEI Mar 2026 "Learning curves", Mar 24, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report)
- Jun 2026: the main text gives no overall automation percentage. Automation share (directive plus feedback-loop) rises with self-reported current and anticipated AI exposure. After controlling for occupation, women's automation share is 7.3pp lower — [AEI Jun 2026 "Cadences", Jun 26, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)

**API and enterprise usage**
- Aug 2025 API: 77% of transcripts were automation and 12% augmentation, against about 50% automation on Claude.ai. 97% of automation-dominant tasks were on the API, against 47% on Claude.ai. Computer & Math was about 44% of API traffic and Office & Admin about 10% — [AEI Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report)
- API cost sensitivity: after task controls, a 1% cost increase goes with a 0.29% drop in usage. "Capability and economic value matter more than cost." Each 1% more input context goes with 0.38% more output, suggesting that access to context is a bottleneck for enterprise deployment — [AEI Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report)
- Nov 2025 API: about three-quarters automation, with a directive share of 64% (against 32% on Claude.ai). API Computer & Math rose from 44% (Aug) to 46% (Nov), and API Office & Admin rose 3pp to 13% — [AEI Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- Feb 2026 API: two workflows at least doubled in share: "business sales and outreach automation" and "automated trading and market operations." Computer & Math share was up 14% on the API and down 18% on Claude.ai since Aug 2025. Anthropic says this migration of coding to the API "may signal more imminent change for associated jobs" — [AEI Mar 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report)

**Occupation and task shares**
- Computer & Math share of Claude.ai peaked at 40% (Mar 2025) and was 34% (Nov 2025). Educational Instruction rose from 9% (Jan 2025) to 15% (Nov 2025). The top single task, "modifying software to correct errors," was 6% of Claude.ai and about 10% of API. Over 3,000 unique O*NET tasks were observed. (Chapter 2 of the same report gives Computer & Math as 36% on Claude.ai and 52% on the API; the report does not reconcile the two figures.) — [AEI Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- Aug 2025 Claude.ai: Business & Financial Operations fell from 6% to 3% and Management from 5% to 3%. Code creation rose from 4.1% to 8.6% while debugging fell from 16.1% to 13.3% — [AEI Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report)
- Feb 2026: Computer & Math was 35% of Claude.ai conversations and Management rose from 3% to 5%. The top-10 task share on Claude.ai fell from 24% to 19%, a sign of diversification. Personal use rose from 35% to 42% and coursework fell from 19% to 12% — [AEI Mar 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report)
- Concentration (Aug 2025): the bottom 80% of tasks account for only 12.7% of Claude.ai usage and 10.5% of API usage. Task Gini is 0.84 for Claude.ai and 0.86 for the API — [AEI Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report)
- Jun 2026 outputs: 93% of conversations produce an artifact. Work conversations break down as documents/reports 20%, explanations 9%, email drafts 7%, and analyses 6%. The share that is work-related is 80% for marketing content, 81% for blogs/articles, and 82% for database queries — [AEI Jun 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)

**Occupational task coverage**
- Earlier, 36% of jobs had AI use on at least 25% of their tasks and about 4% on at least 75%. Combined across reports, 49% of jobs reach 25%+ task coverage — [AEI Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report). By Feb 2026 the 49% was "barely changed" — [AEI Mar 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report)
- "Effective AI coverage" weights coverage by success and time. Occupations with about 90% task coverage often fall below the 45° line, so raw coverage overstates impact. Data entry keyers rank high, radiologists and medical transcriptionists move up, and microbiologists fall below the line — [AEI Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- Deskilling: Claude-covered tasks average 14.4 years of required schooling, against 13.2 for all O*NET tasks. Removing them therefore deskills many occupations; technical writers and travel agents deskill, while real estate managers upskill — [AEI Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)

**Task success, time savings and autonomy (directly useful for task-level calibration)**
- Success rates are 67% on Claude.ai and 49% on the API. By domain, personal tasks reach 78% and software development 61%. On Claude.ai, success is about 70% for tasks requiring less than high-school education and 66% for college-level tasks. On the API, success is about 60% for sub-hour tasks and about 45% for tasks of 5+ hours. The 50% success horizon is about 3.5 hours on the API and an extrapolated ~19 hours on Claude.ai (for comparison, METR gives about 2 hours for Sonnet 4.5 and about 5 hours for Opus 4.5) — [AEI Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- Time: Claude.ai tasks take 3.1 human-only hours against about 15 minutes with AI. API tasks take 1.7 hours against 5 minutes. The speedup is about 9x for tasks needing 12 years of schooling and about 12x for 16 years — [AEI Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- Implied productivity: +1.8pp annual labor-productivity growth over a decade at baseline; +1.0 to 1.2pp after success adjustment; +0.6 to 0.9pp with complementarity (CES σ=0.5) — [AEI Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- Autonomy (1 to 5 scale): software development and personal clusters both average about 3.5, and API autonomy is uniformly lower than Claude.ai — [AEI Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report). In Jun 2026, Claude Code showed higher autonomy than chat in 26 of 31 output types, an average gap of 0.37 points. Autonomy and compute correlate at r=0.68 — [AEI Jun 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)
- Tenure: users who signed up 6+ months earlier have about 5pp higher success unadjusted, or 3 to 4pp with task controls. They also do about 7pp more work use — [AEI Mar 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report)
- The average hourly wage of Claude.ai tasks fell from $49.3 to $47.9 and required education fell from 12.2 to 11.9 years (Nov 2025 → Feb 2026) — [AEI Mar 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report)

**Worker survey (Apr to Jun 2026, about 9,700 linked respondents; skewed toward tech)**
- Computer & Math workers are about 30% of respondents, against 4% of U.S. employment. Close to 6 in 10 expect higher AI task exposure next year, and over one-third expect AI to handle "most or nearly all" of their tasks next year. More than one-third see significant change to their responsibilities as likely. 10% rate losing their own job as likely, against a BLS annualized layoff rate of about 13.4%. Respondents with 15+ years of experience report about 10pp less AI-doable work than first-year workers. Reported gains: speed 86%, scope 82%, quality 69%, cost savings 27% — [AEI Jun 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)

**Geography (U.S.)**
- Aug 2025 Anthropic AI Usage Index (AUI): DC 3.82, Utah 3.78 (flagged for possible abuse), California 2.13, New York 1.58, Virginia 1.57. California holds 25.3% of U.S. usage. Across states, 1% higher GDP per capita goes with 1.8% higher AUI. The U.S. has 21.6% of global usage — [AEI Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report)
- Nov 2025: the top five states hold 50% of usage with 38% of the working-age population. The U.S. state Gini fell from 0.37 to 0.32. 1% higher tech-worker share goes with 0.36% higher usage per capita. Implied parity was 2 to 5 years — [AEI Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- Feb 2026: the top-5 states' share of per-person usage fell from 30% to 24% (Aug 2025 → Feb 2026). Convergence slowed, and time to near-parity was revised to 5 to 9 years — [AEI Mar 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report)

### Inferences
- For a task-scoring model, the AEI suggests discounting raw "AI can do it" by success rate (about 50 to 67%) and by task length. Long (5h+) multi-step tasks succeed less than half the time on the API.
- The automation share is not monotonically rising on consumer chat. It moved 27% → 39% → 32% (directive). Automation is shifting to the API and Claude Code channels, so chat-only data understates delegation.
- Coverage is broad (49% of occupations at 25%+ of tasks) but shallow: 80% of tasks get only about 11 to 13% of usage.

### Gaps
- No overall automation/augmentation split was published for the Feb 2026 or Jun 2026 data in the main text; it may be in the appendices or the HuggingFace dataset (https://huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/EconomicIndex).
- The Oct 16, 2026 "How employment is changing at Anthropic" report was seen only through secondary coverage (see Section 4).

## 2. Microsoft "Working with AI" (Copilot AI applicability scores)

### Takeaway
On 200k Bing Copilot conversations, information work (gathering information, writing, communicating, teaching/explaining) is both the most common and the most successful AI-assisted activity. Applicability is highest for Media & Communication, Sales Representatives (Services), Information & Record Clerks, Mathematical Science and Computer occupations (scores of about 0.29 to 0.38). Physical and care occupations score 0.03 to 0.07. The score correlates strongly with Eloundou exposure (r=0.73) but only weakly with wages (r=0.13).

### Cited Findings
- Tomlinson, Jaffe, Wang, Counts, Suri, "Working with AI: Measuring the Applicability of Generative AI to Occupations," arXiv:2507.07935. First version Jul 10, 2025; latest revision v7 Oct 23, 2026. Based on 200k anonymized Bing Copilot conversations over 9 months, classified to O*NET Intermediate Work Activities (IWAs). Occupation-level applicability rose over the 9 months, mainly because tasks became more varied. Data is at github.com/microsoft/working-with-ai — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07935)
- User goals vs. AI actions: the user-goal IWA set and the AI-action IWA set are disjoint in 40% of conversations. Each user-goal IWA maps to about 2 AI-performed IWAs. The most common user goals are information gathering/learning and communicating, followed by teaching/explaining and writing. The AI mostly acts by communicating and teaching/explaining. The highest success (completion × scope) is in communicating, teaching/explaining and writing; the lowest is in image generation and data analysis — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07935)
- Minor-group applicability scores, highest: Media & Communication Workers 0.38; Sales Reps, Services 0.35; Information & Record Clerks 0.33; Mathematical Science 0.32; Computer Occupations 0.29; Business Operations Specialists 0.24; Financial Specialists 0.23. Lowest: Forest & Conservation 0.03; Plant & System Operators 0.04; Grounds Maintenance 0.04; Home Health Aides & Nursing Assistants 0.04; Extraction 0.05; Construction Trades 0.07; Material Moving 0.07 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07935)
- Correlations (employment-weighted): r=0.73 with Eloundou et al. exposure and r=0.13 with wage — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07935)
- Top-ranked occupations include Interpreters & Translators, Historians, Writers & Authors, Customer Service Reps, CNC tool programmers, telephone operators and journalists. Phlebotomists and nursing assistants are among the lowest. Media coverage reports that 98% of interpreters' activities overlap with frequent Copilot tasks (secondary, Jul/Aug 2025) — [TNND/WJLA](https://wjla.com/news/nation-world/interpreter-historian-top-list-of-40-jobs-with-high-ai-overlap-microsoft-report)
- The authors caution that the scores are not forecasts of job displacement (secondary quote from co-author Kiran Tomlinson) — [TNND/WJLA](https://wjla.com/news/nation-world/interpreter-historian-top-list-of-40-jobs-with-high-ai-overlap-microsoft-report)

### Inferences
- The gap between user goals and AI actions (AI "performs" communicating and explaining while the human "does" the underlying task) supports modeling each task as human-goal plus AI-subtask, rather than as fully substitutable.
- White-collar minor groups cluster at 0.23 to 0.38 on a 0 to 1 scale. Even the most exposed groups are far below full applicability once frequency, completion and scope are combined.

### Gaps
- The 2026 revisions (v6 Dec 2025, v7 Oct 23, 2026) may contain updated scores. I read only partial text and did not obtain the exact top-40 occupation scores or IWA-level shares, which are in figures. The GitHub data has them.
- I found no separate 2026 Microsoft follow-up paper, beyond the v7 revision.

## 3. Theoretical exposure estimates (Eloundou "GPTs are GPTs", McKinsey, Goldman Sachs, newer task-level indices)

### Takeaway
Capability-based estimates converge on roughly half to 60% of U.S. work time being technically addressable by current AI. Estimates: McKinsey (Nov 2025) puts 57% of work hours as automatable with today's agents and robots, 44% of them by agents. Anthropic (Sep 2026) puts about half of work exposed to LLMs alone and about 80% with robots. ESADE (Oct 2026) finds 37.8% of time replaceable and 48.4% augmentable. Displacement estimates are much smaller: Goldman puts it at 6 to 7% of workers over a decade.

### Cited Findings
- **Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin, Rock (OpenAI/UPenn).** About 80% of U.S. workers could have at least 10% of their tasks affected by GPTs, and about 19% at least 50% of tasks. Exposure rises with income. arXiv:2303.10130, Mar 17, 2023, revised through v5 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130v2). As summarized in ESADE (Oct 2026): GPT-4 rated α (direct exposure) at 14.2%, β at 34.5% and γ at 54.8% across occupations, with human-rated β at 30.3% — [ESADE WP](https://www.esade.edu/ecpol/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/working_paper_2026-10-22.pdf). (A journal version is often cited as Science 2024; I did not verify the venue.)
- **McKinsey Global Institute, "Agents, robots, and us: Skill partnerships in the age of AI" (Nov 25, 2025).** Currently demonstrated technology could in theory automate 57% of U.S. work hours: agents 44% and robots 13%. Capturing it would require workflow redesign and is worth about $2.9 trillion of U.S. value by 2030. More than 70% of employer-sought skills are used in both automatable and non-automatable work. The 57% is technical potential, not job loss — [McKinsey press release (DE), Dec 2, 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/de/news/presse/2025-12-02-mgi-agents-and-robots); [Fortune, Nov 25, 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/11/25/why-ai-wont-take-your-job-partnership-agents-robots-mckinsey/)
- **Goldman Sachs Research (Aug 2025).** AI could displace 6 to 7% of the U.S. workforce if widely adopted, within a range of 3 to 14%. If today's use cases spread economy-wide, 2.5% of U.S. employment is at risk. Peak unemployment impact is about 0.5pp, and the transition runs over about a decade. Higher-risk roles: programmers, accountants/auditors, legal and admin assistants, customer service reps. Goldman's earlier 2023 estimate was "300M jobs exposed / about a quarter of work tasks automatable" (not re-verified here) — [Goldman Sachs Insights](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/how-will-ai-affect-the-global-workforce); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/goldman-sachs-ai-jobs/)
- **Anthropic "What work can robots do?" (Sep 30, 2026).**
  - U.S. working time splits into 54% cognitive/interpersonal and 46% physical.
  - Robots can perform 74% of physical tasks, which are 34% of working hours. By setting, as a share of all work time: 23% in purpose-built environments, 10% in structured workplaces, 1% in unstructured environments, and 12% that robots cannot do.
  - Robots are cost-competitive for only 0.3% of tasks. At a 3% annual cost decline it would take about 40 years to reach 10%.
  - About half of work is exposed to LLMs alone and 81% to LLMs plus robots. No occupation group is below 40% exposed. Nursing and general repair are largely unexposed.
  - Barriers to robot automation, as shares of physical tasks: capability about 70% (manipulation about half), human preferences about 25%, regulation 14%.
  — [Anthropic, Sep 30, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/what-work-can-robots-do)
- **ESADE EcPol (Contreras, Galindo, Gómez, Hidalgo), "Replace, augment, support: a nested task-level measure of generative-AI exposure for the full O*NET catalogue."** First version Mar 15, 2026; this version Oct 22, 2026. Three frontier LLMs plus a Delphi round rate all 24,087 task-activity pairs of O*NET 31.0 (923 occupations) against 33 capability benchmarks.
  - Time-weighted, the average occupation has 37.8% of working time replaceable, 48.4% augmentable and 79.2% supportable. Time weights are ONS time weights, a UK source, so treat U.S. time allocation as approximate.
  - Augmentation correlates r=0.89 with Eloundou β.
  - 99.9% of Claude.ai use falls on tasks rated supportable and 84.7% on tasks rated replaceable.
  — [ESADE WP, Oct 22, 2026](https://www.esade.edu/ecpol/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/working_paper_2026-10-22.pdf)
- **ESADE major-group results (share of working time; Re = replaceable, Su = supportable, C3–C4 = tacit expert judgement):**

- **Upjohn Institute (Horrigan & Vasilauskas).** They rated 2,087 O*NET Detailed Work Activities. Documentation, data analysis, computer work, scheduling and information processing rank most exposed. Nearly all legal and business/financial operations employment falls in the top exposure quartile (date not confirmed; secondary summary) — [Upjohn](https://www.upjohn.org/horrigan-returns-bls-present-ai-and-future-work-research)

### Inferences
- Several sources give an occupation-agnostic prior for a white-collar task-scoring model: about 60 to 70% of time replaceable in Computer/Math, Business/Financial and Office/Admin; about 50% in Legal and Management; about 30 to 40% in healthcare practitioner, education and social service work. The sources are ESADE (Re layer) and McKinsey (agents at 44% of all hours, higher in office work).
- The gap between technical potential (about 50 to 60% of hours) and observed displacement (Section 4) is large. Calibration should separate "can do" from "is used for" from "replaces labor."

### Gaps
- I could not open the McKinsey primary report (HTTP 503), so occupation-archetype splits (people-centric, agent-centric, robot-centric employment shares) are missing.
- I did not locate any 2026 OpenAI exposure update, IMF U.S.-specific 2026 update, or Brookings 2026 exposure estimate.
- I did not verify a 2026 Goldman update.

## 4. Employment effects: Stanford "Canaries", Anthropic observed exposure, Yale Budget Lab, NY Fed, 2026 evidence

### Takeaway
There is no economy-wide AI displacement as of mid-to-late 2026. Hiring of young workers (ages 22 to 25) into automation-heavy, AI-exposed occupations is falling, and the gap is widening: it was 13% in the Aug 2025 Stanford paper, 15% at the Jul 2025 data vintage, and 19% by Jun 2026. The effect runs through reduced hiring rather than layoffs, and is concentrated where AI automates rather than augments.

### Cited Findings
- **Stanford DEL "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" (Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen; ADP payroll), Aug 12, 2026 update with data through Jun 2026.**
  - Employment of 22 to 25 year olds in highly AI-exposed occupations is about 19% below where it would be had it kept pace with less-exposed peers. The gap was 15% at the Jul 2025 vintage.
  - In levels, from Nov 2022 to Jun 2026, employment of 22 to 25 year olds fell about 11% in the two most-exposed quintiles and grew about 10% in the three least-exposed.
  - Exposure is measured with Eloundou et al. (2024); the results reportedly hold under alternative measures.
  - Declines are concentrated where observed AI use mostly automates. Where AI complements, employment is flat or rising, especially for experienced workers.
  - The adjustment runs via reduced hiring, not separations, and shows up in employment rather than base pay.
  - The gap shrinks when education is controlled for. ADP gaps are larger than in national surveys, with the discrepancy concentrated in education, health care and public administration.
  - The authors describe the results as descriptive, not causal.
  — [Stanford DEL, Aug 12, 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)
- The original Aug 2025 version reported a 13% relative employment decline for ages 22 to 25 in exposed jobs, with data through Jul 2025 (secondary summary) — [Edward Conard summary](https://www.edwardconard.com/macro-roundup/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent/). The Anthropic paper cites the original as estimating a 6 to 16% fall — [Anthropic, Mar 5, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts)
- **Anthropic, "Labor market impacts of AI: A new measure and early evidence" (Massenkoff & McCrory), Mar 5, 2026.**
  - Method: "observed exposure" combines Eloundou β with AEI usage. Automated use gets full weight and augmentative use half weight; tasks are time-weighted within occupations.
  - Theoretical vs. observed: Computer & Math is 94% feasible by β but 33% observed. Office & Admin is 90% feasible by β.
  - Most exposed occupations: Computer Programmers 75%, then Customer Service Reps, then Data Entry Keyers 67%.
  - 30% of workers have zero observed coverage.
  - Each +10pp of coverage goes with 0.6pp lower BLS-projected growth over 2024 to 2034 (β alone shows no correlation).
  - The top exposure quartile, relative to the zero-exposure group, is 16pp more female, earns 47% more, and has more graduate degrees (17.4% vs. 4.5%).
  - There is no systematic rise in unemployment for exposed workers since late 2022; the minimum detectable effect is about 1pp.
  - Ages 22 to 25: the job-finding rate into high-exposure occupations falls about 0.5pp/month, a 14% drop relative to 2022 that is barely significant. There is no comparable drop for workers over 25.
  — [Anthropic, Mar 5, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts)
- **Anthropic internal case study "How employment is changing at Anthropic" (Oct 16, 2026; secondary coverage only).**
  - The junior share of technical hires fell from 27% (early 2025) to 9% (Jun 2026), while technical hiring still grew in absolute terms.
  - Interactive agent hours outnumbered employee hours 6:1 by August 2026.
  - Code commits per engineer were about 5x their level six months earlier.
  - Coverage cautions that this is a single AI firm, not economy-wide evidence.
  — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/anthropic-ai-jobs-study-work-changing); [LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/anthropic-tripled-juniors-27-percent-tech-hires-to-9)
- **Yale Budget Lab (with Brookings).**
  - The original analysis (Oct 2025) found that the shares of workers in low, middle and high exposure buckets have been stable since early 2023. The occupational mix is changing slightly faster than in the past, but the change predates generative AI.
  - A Feb 2026 piece found "no clear indication" of labor-market effects and flagged possible "AI-washing" of layoffs.
  - A later 2026 update, using March 2026 CPS data and Anthropic Feb 2026 usage, found no substantial change. The one uptick is in the dissimilarity of occupational mix between older and younger college graduates, still within the historical range.
  — [Yale Budget Lab tracker](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/node/1419/pdf); [Yale Budget Lab Feb 2026](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/node/1495/pdf); [Fortune, Feb 2, 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/02/02/ai-labor-market-yale-budget-lab-ai-washing/)
- **NY Fed regional surveys.**
  - In 2025, among AI-using service firms, 1% made AI-induced layoffs and 13% expected to. 23% expected to hire fewer workers, up from 12%, with hiring cuts concentrated in jobs needing a college degree.
  - 35% of AI-using service firms are retraining workers, up from 24%.
  - Manufacturers reported no AI layoffs.
  — [Liberty Street Economics, Sep 4, 2025](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/tag/artificial-intelligence/)
  - An Aug 5, 2026 summary of NY Fed "Street Level" findings says firms report very few AI-driven layoffs but are changing hiring plans and skill requirements, especially for college-educated roles (secondary) — [CDBanks summary](https://www.cdbanks.org/news/ais-impact-labor-and-hiring)

### Inferences
- The consistent 2026 signal is entry-level hiring compression in automation-heavy white-collar occupations (software, customer service, data entry), not layoffs of incumbents. A model should treat early-career, codified tasks as the first margin of labor substitution.
- Experienced workers in the same occupations show no gap. That is consistent with tacit or expert judgement tasks being complements (see Section 6).

### Gaps
- I did not find the NY Fed 2026 survey numbers on the primary site.
- I did not find BLS/JOLTS-based 2026 white-collar employment evidence attributed to AI, beyond the studies above.
- The Stanford monthly "Canaries Dashboard" was not checked for Jul to Oct 2026 values.

## 5. O*NET-based time allocation across work activities for white-collar occupations

### Takeaway
I found no published study giving O*NET Generalized/Intermediate Work Activity time shares (communication, analysis, interpersonal, physical) for U.S. white-collar occupations. The best available proxies are three:
- Anthropic: 54% of U.S. working time is cognitive/interpersonal and 46% physical.
- ESADE: 50% of O*NET task-activity pairs are purely informational and 12% require presence.
- Microsoft: information gathering, communicating, writing and teaching/explaining dominate AI-relevant activity.

### Cited Findings
- U.S. working time is 54% cognitive and interpersonal and 46% physical; physical tasks are 34% of working hours that robots could in principle do — [Anthropic, Sep 30, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/what-work-can-robots-do)
- ESADE physical bands over O*NET 31.0 task-activity pairs. "Share" is the share of all pairs; "Re" is the share of pairs in that band rated replaceable.

  | Band | Share of pairs | Re |
  |---|---|---|
  | D0 purely informational | 50.1% | 70.5% |
  | D1 needs presence | 12.2% | 14.1% |
  | D2 manipulation | 15.6% | 0.5% |
  | D3 | 19.4% | 0.0% |
  | D4 | 2.7% | 0.0% |

  37.7% of pairs need manipulation (D2+) — [ESADE WP Table 8, Oct 22, 2026](https://www.esade.edu/ecpol/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/working_paper_2026-10-22.pdf)
- Codifiability bands, by the same reading: C1 (rule-based) is 44.4% of pairs with 33.7% replaceable. C2 (judgement among defensible alternatives) is 39.8% of pairs with 50.5% replaceable. C3 (tacit expert judgement) is 14.6% of pairs with 13.7% replaceable but 47.7% augmentable. C4 is 0.9% of pairs with 0% replaceable and 74.4% augmentable. The average occupation spends 15.0% of its time at C3/C4 — [ESADE WP Table 9](https://www.esade.edu/ecpol/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/working_paper_2026-10-22.pdf)
- Microsoft's Copilot data: the most common user-goal IWAs are information gathering and communicating, then teaching/explaining and writing — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07935)
- Pew (Jul 26, 2023) rated 41 O*NET work activities as low, medium or high AI exposure and weighted them by importance (not time) to occupations, using O*NET v27.3 — [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2023/07/26/which-u-s-workers-are-more-exposed-to-ai-on-their-jobs/)
- Anthropic's observed-exposure measure time-weights tasks within each occupation, using O*NET task importance/frequency — [Anthropic, Mar 5, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts)

### Inferences
- For white-collar occupations, the D0 share is likely well above the all-occupation 50% average. ESADE's Office/Admin and Computer/Math groups are about 92 to 100% "supportable," which implies very little physical time. This is an inference; no direct time shares were found.

### Gaps
- No study found reports minutes or percent of time by GWA/IWA for white-collar SOC groups. O*NET itself does not publish time shares by GWA, only importance/level ratings and task "frequency" categories. Building this would require O*NET task frequency × task-to-DWA/IWA mapping, or ATUS/time-diary data. Candidate sources not reviewed: Microsoft's GitHub IWA data and the Gao & Huang micro-action paper (arXiv 2606.07939).

## 6. Task types that remain hard: in-person, physical, relationship, management, accountability

### Takeaway
Hard-to-automate work clusters into four groups: physical manipulation, in-person presence, tacit expert judgement, and work bundled to a responsible person (relationships, organisational accountability, authorship). Even in exposed occupations, AI is far more often an augmenter than a replacer for these tasks, and cost (for robots) and preferences or regulation remain binding.

### Cited Findings
- Replacement probability falls from 70.5% for purely informational tasks to 14.1% when presence is required and 0.5% when manipulation is required — [ESADE WP Table 8](https://www.esade.edu/ecpol/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/working_paper_2026-10-22.pdf)
- Tacit expert judgement (C3) is 13.7% replaceable but 47.7% augmentable. "Expert complementarity," time where AI helps an expert without replacing them, averages 12.1% of working time per occupation (median 3.8%). By group it is highest in Legal (31.5%), Life Sciences (29.4%), Community & Social Service (26.8%) and Healthcare practitioners (26.5%), and lowest in Office/Admin (0.8%).
  - The paper also defines "bundling factors" that keep tasks with a person: relational nexus, organisational responsibility and authorship. These are nearly uncorrelated with exposure (|ρ| ≤ 0.13), so they carry independent information.
  - Example, family physicians: 29.9% of time is replaceable (records, orders, referrals) and 44.2% is expert complementarity (diagnosis, prescribing). Immunizing patients is outside all three layers.
  — [ESADE WP, Oct 22, 2026](https://www.esade.edu/ecpol/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/working_paper_2026-10-22.pdf)
- Occupations that physically work with people score lowest in Copilot applicability: Home Health Aides/Nursing Assistants 0.04, Construction 0.07, Food Prep 0.15 — [Microsoft arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07935)
- Physical tasks: robots are cost-competitive for 0.3%, only about 2% of physical tasks are feasible in unstructured settings, manipulation is the binding capability gap for about half of physical tasks, and human preferences constrain about a quarter — [Anthropic, Sep 30, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/what-work-can-robots-do)
- On AI-usage platforms, Management is only 3 to 5% of Claude.ai conversations (rising from 3% to 5% by Feb 2026) — [AEI Mar 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report). Employment falls only where AI automates; where it complements, experienced workers' employment is flat or rising — [Stanford DEL, Aug 12, 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)
- Coverage overstates impact: occupations with about 90% task coverage often have much lower success- and time-weighted "effective coverage." Tasks of 5+ hours succeed about 45% of the time on the API — [AEI Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- Experienced workers (15+ years) report about 10pp less AI-doable work than first-year workers — [AEI Jun 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)
- McKinsey (Nov 2025): more than 70% of employer-sought skills are used in both automatable and non-automatable work. The firm frames the future as human–agent–robot "partnerships" that need human guidance — [Fortune, Nov 25, 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/11/25/why-ai-wont-take-your-job-partnership-agents-robots-mckinsey/)

### Inferences
- A task-scoring model could use multipliers rather than a single capability score:
  - Physical/presence requirement: strong discount, near 0 for manipulation.
  - Codifiability: C2 is the most replaceable band, C3/C4 augment-only.
  - Bundling factors (relationship, accountability, authorship): limit substitution even when capability is high.
  - Task length and complexity: a success discount.
- Management and accountability tasks show low observed usage and high support, but low replacement. They are best modeled as AI-supported, not AI-performed.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative estimate isolating "accountability/liability" as a barrier for white-collar tasks beyond ESADE's bundling factors and Anthropic's robot regulation share (14% of physical tasks).
- I found no direct measurement of AI success rates on management or interpersonal tasks.
